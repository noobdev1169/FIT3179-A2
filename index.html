The Missing Days

How often Australian students go to school, who misses out, and what makes the difference (2015–2025)

By [Your name] · October 2026 · Data: ACARA, Australian Bureau of Statistics, NSW Department of Education

1

How many?

Almost 4 in 10

students in Years 1–10 missed more than a day of school a fortnight in the first half of 2025.

That is about 1.24 million students, around 400,000 more than in 2019, before COVID-19. In 2022 it was half of all students.

The national attendance rate still looks high, at 88.8%. But that average hides how many students regularly miss school, and who they are.

How attendance is measured

Attendance rate
The share of school days that students attended in Semester 1. In 2025 it was 88.8%, so students missed about 1 day in 9.
Attendance level
The share of students who attended at least 90% of days. In 2025 it was 62.1%.
Missing out (this page)
Everyone else: students who missed more than 1 day in 10, about a day a fortnight. In 2025, 37.9% (100 − 62.1).
2

When?

Absence was already creeping up before COVID-19. In 2022, the worst year in a decade of national records, half of all students missed more than a day a fortnight. Attendance has improved since, but in 2025 every year level was still missing more school than in 2019.

The pattern by age barely changes: every year, absence jumps in Year 8 and keeps rising to Year 10.

3

Where?

The further students live from a major city, the more school they miss. In 2025, just over a third of students in major cities missed more than a day a fortnight (35.2%), compared with three in four in very remote areas (74.7%).

But a map coloured by area exaggerates the outback, where few people live. Sizing each area by its students tells the other half of the story: most students who miss out live in cities.

Notes and caveats

  • Attendance covers full-time students in Years 1–10 in Semester 1. ACARA did not publish 2020 data because of COVID-19.
  • NSW changed how it calculates government school attendance in 2018 and 2021, so comparisons with earlier years need care.
  • Student numbers that combine ABS counts with ACARA attendance shares are estimates.
